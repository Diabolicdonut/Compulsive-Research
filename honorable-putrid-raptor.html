I · The card

A Face-Down Threat Fed the Coming Resurrection

Putrid Raptor deserves a place in the Zombies shelf because it is a morph creature that turns an expendable Zombie card into a sudden 4/4 and places material in the graveyard for Bidding. The card belongs to the same Onslaught Block ecosystem as Carrion Feeder, Rotlung Reanimator, Gempalm Polluter, and Patriarch's Bidding, but it solves a distinct part of the archetype’s problem.

The attraction is not six mana for a 4/4. It is a face-down 2/2 whose morph cost discards a Zombie instead of mana, allowing the deck to develop a body and preload a future Patriarch’s Bidding.

The submitted mono-black list provides the right lens. It contains thirty creatures, six noncreature spells, and twenty-four lands, aiming to play early Zombies, trade them profitably, and then use Bidding, Polluter, Muse, or Noxious Ghoul to convert creature density into a decisive turn. This article evaluates Putrid Raptor inside that plan and within the broader history of Zombie Bidding.

II · The debut

Born inside Magic’s dedicated tribal block

Putrid Raptor first appeared in Scourge in 2003, with artwork by Pete Venters. Onslaught Block was built to make creature type matter at nearly every level: bodies carried overlapping types, cycling cards rewarded a specific tribe, and even lands such as Unholy Grotto participated in the theme.

The result was a Zombie card pool with unusually clear internal roles. One-drops established sacrifice fodder, Zombie Clerics linked to Rotlung Reanimator, lords compressed the curve, Gempalm Polluter supplied uncounterable reach, and Patriarch’s Bidding offered mass recursion. Putrid Raptor entered not as an isolated design but as one gear in that machine.

Contemporary Zombie Bidding lists experimented with three copies, especially blue builds using Read the Runes and Twisted Abomination to make graveyard setup a deliberate plan. In a straightforward mono-black aggro shell, the discard cost is harder to recover.

III · The archetype

Zombie Bidding is an engine deck wearing an aggro deck’s skin

The submitted list can attack normally, especially when Undead Warchief turns small creatures into real threats. Its deeper identity, however, is recursive aggro-control. Carrion Feeder converts doomed bodies into permanent power; Rotlung Reanimator replaces dying Clerics; Withered Wretch removes the opponent’s graveyard; and Patriarch's Bidding returns the Zombie half of the exchange.

Noxious Ghoul is the bridge from recursion to board control. When several Zombies return together, each entrance gives non-Zombies -1/-1, often clearing the opposing battlefield before combat. Gempalm Polluter then turns the surviving body count into direct life loss while replacing itself.

Putrid Raptor matters to the extent that it improves one of those transitions: setup into exchange, exchange into graveyard, graveyard into Bidding, or Bidding into a lethal board. That functional test is more useful than simply counting how many cards say “Zombie.”

IV · Rules

The exact wording determines the line

The current Oracle text reads: “Morph—Discard a Zombie card. (You may cast this card face down as a 2/2 creature for {3}. Turn it face up any time for its morph cost.)” The effect should be read alongside the rules for sacrifice costs, morph, characteristic-defining abilities, graveyard targets, and simultaneous zone changes as applicable.

Patriarch’s Bidding returns all chosen creature cards simultaneously. Noxious Ghoul then triggers once for itself and once for every other Zombie that entered. Rotlung Reanimator does not trigger for every Zombie death; it triggers for itself and other Clerics, which makes Withered Wretch and Shepherd of Rot especially important dual-type cards.

Cast it face down when the hidden information matters or when a discarded Zombie has a clear Bidding destination. Do not discard the only Warchief or Noxious Ghoul merely to win ordinary combat; the morph should prepare a larger swing.

V · The role

What this slot contributes to the sixty

In this exact shell, Putrid Raptor is a morph creature that turns an expendable Zombie card into a sudden 4/4 and places material in the graveyard for Bidding. It should be measured by the turns it improves rather than by its best imagined battlefield.

Treat it as a specialized Bidding enabler, not a default four-of. One sideboard or flex copy is reasonable; multiple copies increase card disadvantage and compete with stronger four- and five-mana Zombies.

The card’s relationship with Graveborn Muse is also important. Muse rewards a high Zombie count but punishes the player for building a board that cannot end the game. The best supporting cards either protect that board, turn it into immediate damage, or let the player sacrifice Muse before the upkeep becomes fatal.

VI · Deck evaluation

A coherent sixty with three structural pressure points

The submitted main deck is exactly sixty cards and has a real plan. Eight one-drops produce early material. Withered Wretch and Rotlung Reanimator occupy the critical two- and three-mana turns. Warchief, Polluter, Muse, Ghoul, and Bidding create multiple ways to turn creature count into inevitability.

The first weakness is the mana. Four Unholy Grottos leave only twenty black-producing lands. In a deck with Withered Wretch at BB, Warchief and Muse at 2BB, and Ghoul or Bidding at 3BB, that is an unnecessary consistency loss. In an eight-card sample, twenty black sources produce at least two black sources about 82% of the time; twenty-two black sources raise that to roughly 87%.

The second weakness is curve congestion: four Warchiefs, three Muses, and three Noxious Ghouls create ten cards costing four or five mana. The third is that Bidding is powerful but the mono-black build lacks deliberate discard or looting. The deck fills its graveyard through combat and sacrifice, which is slower but less card-disadvantageous than morph-discard or blue draw spells.

VII · The candidate cards

Shepherd belongs closest to the main deck

Of the trailing cards, two copies of Shepherd of Rot are the strongest addition. Shepherd is a Zombie Cleric, so it strengthens Rotlung Reanimator while giving a stalled board a repeatable damage engine. The life loss is symmetrical, which means sequencing and race math matter, but the Zombie deck normally chooses when to activate it.

Soulless One is best treated as a sideboard threat for attrition, removal-heavy matchups, and Zombie mirrors. It grows from every Zombie on the battlefield and every Zombie card in all graveyards, but it adds another four-drop without evasion or immediate value.

Putrid Raptor is more specialized. Discarding a Zombie to turn a face-down creature into a 4/4 can preload Bidding, but it is genuine card disadvantage. The card was more convincing in historical blue Zombie Bidding builds that already used Read the Runes and Twisted Abomination to make graveyard setup part of the plan.

VIII · Construction

The cleanest revision changes only four slots

The conservative tune is to remove one Graveborn Muse and one Noxious Ghoul for two Shepherds of Rot, then replace two Unholy Grottos with two Barren Moors. Creature count, spell count, and land count remain unchanged at thirty, six, and twenty-four.

This revision retains the submitted deck’s identity. It lowers the average cost, increases the number of Clerics for Rotlung, provides another direct-damage route, and raises the number of black-producing lands from twenty to twenty-two. Barren Moor enters tapped, but later copies cycle when the deck is looking for Bidding or Polluter.

The recommendation is not that the original list is wrong. It is that the deck’s strongest synergies already exist, so the best tuning improves the mana and curve instead of adding more expensive payoff cards.

IX · Sequencing

Play toward the second battlefield

Zombie Bidding games effectively have two battlefields: the creatures currently in play and the creatures being stored for the recursion turn. Early attacks and sacrifices should be judged by what they accomplish in both places.

When Carrion Feeder is available, sacrifice creatures that would otherwise be exiled or stolen. When Withered Wretch is active, spend spare mana on the opponent’s graveyard before your own end step. Hold Gempalm Polluter until cycling is meaningful unless a 4/3 creature is genuinely the best use of six mana.

Cast it face down when the hidden information matters or when a discarded Zombie has a clear Bidding destination. Do not discard the only Warchief or Noxious Ghoul merely to win ordinary combat; the morph should prepare a larger swing.

X · Matchups

Control is the natural prey; Goblins force the test

Historical commentary described Zombie Bidding as devastating against control because spot removal and sweepers stocked the graveyard for Patriarch’s Bidding. Withered Wretch also attacked Eternal Dragon recursion and other graveyard value.

Goblins was the harder structural matchup. Its own Warchief starts one turn earlier, its creatures produce immediate tempo, and Goblin Bidding can exploit the same mass-reanimation card. Festering Goblin, Infest, and Noxious Ghoul help, but a five-mana answer may arrive after Goblins has already established lethal pressure.

Against other creature decks, the key question is whether Bidding plus Noxious Ghoul becomes a one-sided sweeper. Against tribal opponents, Withered Wretch must remove the relevant creature type before the sorcery resolves.

XI · Counterplay

Attack the graveyard, the mana, or the payoff turn

Remove the face-down creature before combat if the 4/4 would dominate, or attack the graveyard so the discarded card never returns. Edict effects also ignore the surprise size.

Graveyard hate is the broadest answer to the deck. It weakens Bidding, Unholy Grotto, Putrid Raptor’s discard plan, and Soulless One at once. Stabilizer attacks Gempalm Polluter and the block’s cycling engines. Enchantment and artifact hate matter less than timing removal around Rotlung Reanimator and Carrion Feeder.

The opponent should also pressure the Zombie player’s life total. Graveborn Muse and Shepherd of Rot turn life into cards or damage, but both become liabilities when the race is already close.

XII · Player memory

Why Onslaught Zombies still feels like a complete deck

“Every creature was either a Zombie, helped Zombies, or needed Zombies to function.”
The design promise remembered from Onslaught Block theme decks and Zombie Bidding

The deck’s appeal is architectural. The one-drops, dual-type Clerics, cycling payoff, tribal land, lord, card-draw engine, sweeper creature, and mass reanimation spell all point toward one another. Players can see the deck working before they know every matchup.

Putrid Raptor deserves an article because it occupies a recognizable place in that architecture. The strongest tribal cards are not always the cards with the largest text boxes. They are the pieces that make the rest of the deck feel intentionally related.

XIV · Artwork

The illustration makes the mechanical role visible

Pete Venters’s original Scourge illustration gives the card a visual identity before the rules text is read. The hybrid anatomy sells both halves of the design: a disguised morph on the table and a larger predatory body waiting underneath.

Onslaught’s Zombies are not visually uniform, but they share a sense that death has become infrastructure. Bodies, graveyards, and landscapes are presented as resources being reorganized by necromancy. That visual language matches a deck where a creature dying is often preparation rather than failure.
